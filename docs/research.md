# A3i frontend research brief

Reviewed 19 October 2026. This is a desk review of five public websites, not participant research or a performance audit. Observations concern publicly retrievable content and information architecture; no conversion results are inferred.

## Decision

A3i needs to explain the job it helps a visitor complete before asking them to understand its product names. Preserve the approved homepage, then validate whether visitors understand Apps, Ants and Agents and can find the appropriate demo path. Research recommendations below are proposals, not permission to redesign implemented features.

## Five benchmark teardowns

### LangChain

Source: https://www.langchain.com/

The site groups its platform around a development lifecycle: build, test, deploy, monitor, iterate and govern. Product links sit beside concrete activities, while customer stories and developer resources provide routes for deeper evaluation. This helps a technical buyer understand where a product fits, although the number of platform and framework names can increase the learning effort for a newcomer.

For A3i, retain the task-to-outcome illustrations and distinct product actions. Explain the portfolio through work examples before relying on brand names. Do not borrow customer metrics or imply the same proof exists for A3i.

### Intercom

Source: https://www.intercom.com/

Navigation separates products, platform capabilities, customer stories and industry routes. Named capabilities such as inbox, tickets and reporting give buyers recognizable starting points. The breadth helps established buyers compare features but can make a first visit feel dense.

For A3i, preserve the product and industry navigation, make a demo the consistent next step, and distinguish the website concierge from the products being sold. The assistant should explain its scope and provide an honest failure state.

### Notion AI

Source: https://www.notion.com/product/ai

The product page places AI within a familiar workspace and offers routes to understand individual capabilities. This gives abstract AI language a recognizable context. A platform-wide story can still leave a buyer needing to determine which capability solves their particular problem.

For A3i, use concrete documents, meeting notes and follow-up work to explain the offering. Label illustrative diagrams so visitors do not mistake them for operational product screenshots.

### Linear

Source: https://linear.app/

The homepage organizes product development around a coherent system rather than a disconnected collection of tools. Product-oriented sections and links let visitors progressively explore the workflow. Its specialist language suits product teams but is less transferable to an SMB owner unfamiliar with that domain.

For A3i, keep a consistent visual hierarchy across the portfolio and let visitors progressively explore examples. Preserve normal scrolling and keyboard access; polish should not depend on motion.

### Zapier

Source: https://zapier.com/

The site connects automation, agents and apps with workflow discovery and integration browsing. This gives visitors several ways to identify relevant work. A large ecosystem also increases navigation and terminology complexity.

For A3i, the nine-example explorer is a useful bridge from a business task to a product discussion. Avoid adding integrations or workflow capabilities that A3i has not confirmed.

## Three provisional personas

These are hypotheses derived from the assignment, not interview findings. Validate them with the five-second and task tests in evaluation.md.

| Persona | Job and decision | Main concern | Information needed | Success journey |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SMB owner | Reduce repetitive administration without a complex rollout | Setup effort, total cost, and whether a human stays in control | Plain-language examples, scope, supported next step, approved pricing information | Understand offering → find a familiar task → ask a question → request a demo |
| Mid-market operations leader | Introduce repeatable automation across an existing workflow | Integration effort, oversight, reliability and responsibility | Apps/Ants/Agents distinction, workflow steps, product scope and verified deployment details | Explore workflow → review relevant product → ask scope questions → submit company and need |
| Investor | Understand the portfolio and the company's business focus | Differentiation, consistency and credibility | Clear product taxonomy, company story, evidence with sources and contact route | Scan positioning → understand portfolio → inspect company material → contact team |

## Findings to validate

1. The motto alone may not explain the offering within five seconds. Test the complete first viewport before proposing copy changes.
2. Assignment names and current product names differ. Obtain approval before renaming or adding a seventh product.
3. Product diagrams explain outcomes but do not prove live functionality. Preserve their illustration labels.
4. A failed assistant can interrupt conversion. Verify the independent demo form as well as chat.
5. An investor needs credible company information; never substitute fabricated metrics or testimonials for evidence.

## Limits

No user interviews, conversion study, competitor accessibility test, or mobile motion comparison was performed for this brief. Sources can change after the review date. The user's approved layout remains the implementation authority.
